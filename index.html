The problem

Sound familiar?

Four things keep families from making hiking a habit. Wander solves all four.

01
Screens are winning
Kids average 7+ hours on devices every day. Parents know it's a problem and feel stuck. The trail is right there — but so is the phone.
02
"Hiking is boring"
There's nothing for kids to do on the trail. Adults hike. Kids endure. By the time you're back at the car, the phones are already out.
03
Where do we even go?
Researching a hike takes 45 minutes. Is it too hard? Is there parking? Is it safe for a 4-year-old? The planning alone kills the momentum.
04
The bar feels too high
"What if we're not prepared?" keeps families at home. Wander removes every excuse — and every bit of friction — between your couch and the trailhead.

Kids activities

Make the trail as exciting
as anything on a screen

Wander turns every hike into an adventure — with activities, achievements, and surprises built into the trail itself.

Draw a picture
Trail-specific sketching prompts keep little hands busy and minds fully present on the trail.
Plant & animal ID
Point your camera at anything. AI identifies it and shares a fact your kid will actually remember forever.
Snack attack
Location-triggered snack breaks with surprise challenges. Unlocks at the creek crossing, halfway point, or summit.
Level up badges
Earn points, unlock badges, climb the family leaderboard. The kids always beat the parents. Always.
Rise for the prize
Complete goals on trail and earn real rewards. Because summits deserve more than just a photo.
Connect the dots
Key spots along the trail — each with a story, a photo, and a hidden geocache waiting to be found.

Our story

Built by someone who lives this

A Seattle dad who got tired of watching his kids reach for their phones the moment a hike ended.

R
Ryan
Founder · Sammamish, WA

Born and raised in Seattle, raising two kids in Sammamish with the Cascades as the backyard. After 25 years in corporate life, I kept noticing the same thing: we'd have a great hike, get back to the car, and the phones would come out immediately. The trail was already forgotten.

Wander started as a simple question — what if the trail itself was as engaging as anything on a screen?
